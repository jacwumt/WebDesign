The basics

Getting Started

Four pieces make the fishing setup: rod, line, tippet, and kebari. A net and wading staff are useful additions.

Rod Line Tippet Kebari Landing Net Wading Staff

Rod

The long telescoping rod casts the line and helps control the fly. The line attaches at the tip instead of running through guides to a reel.

Line

The main line carries the fly during the cast. I prefer PVC-coated or furled line. A fixed line means the amount of line stays the same while you fish.

Tippet

This thin section connects your main line to the fly. Match its strength to the rod maker's recommendation so the tippet can serve as the weaker link in the setup.

Kebari

Kebari are artificial flies used in Japanese fly fishing. Thread and feathers suggest an insect, while the way you present the fly brings it to life. Patterns can have forward-facing, upright, or swept-back hackle.

Landing Net

A net helps bring a fish under control for unhooking. It is a useful accessory rather than part of the rod-and-line connection.

Wading Staff

A staff provides another point of contact with the streambed when moving through the water. It is an accessory to consider for the places you fish.

How it connects

The main line attaches to the rod tip. Tippet connects that line to the fly. The reel-free setup is what makes tenkara so compact.

  1. Check the rod maker's instructions for attaching your line.
  2. Connect the main line, tippet, and kebari.
  3. Extend the rod gently, keeping the fine tip clear of snags.
  4. Check the connections before your first cast.
