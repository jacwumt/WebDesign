The approach

About Tenkara

Fly fishing with a long rod, a fixed line, and no reel.

Roots in Japan's mountain streams

Tenkara developed among anglers who caught fish for a living in Japan's mountain streams. A simple rod, line, and fly let them work the water without a reel.

Today's telescoping rods make the equipment easier to carry, while the basic connection between rod, line, and fly remains. Tenkara USA helped introduce the method to a wider audience outside Japan in 2009.

Read more: Tenkara USA on tenkara's origins and its introduction outside Japan .

Two ways to fly fish

Western Fly Fishing vs. Tenkara

Both use an artificial fly. The biggest difference is how you manage the line and reach the fish.

Western fly fishing

The setup
A fly rod, reel, fly line, leader, tippet, and fly.
Where it helps
You can let out or retrieve line to change your reach. Different rods and lines give you options for larger water and different fish.
The trade-off
There is more equipment to choose and more loose line to manage while casting and fishing.
Fighting a fish
The reel stores line and, depending on the setup, provides drag for a running fish.

Tenkara

The setup
A telescoping rod, a fixed length of line, tippet, and fly. No reel.
Where it helps
The compact setup travels easily. A long rod helps place the fly and keep line off the water in nearby currents.
The trade-off
Your reach is limited by the rod and line length. Fish beyond that range require a different position or setup.
Fighting a fish
You manage the fish with the rod and your position. There is no reel to release extra line.

The best fit depends on the water and the kind of fishing you enjoy.

A few common questions

Is tenkara still fly fishing?

Yes. The fly and its presentation are central to both approaches. Tenkara uses a fixed line in place of a reel.

What is a kebari?

A kebari is an artificial fly. Tenkara patterns vary, and not every kebari has the same feather shape. The kebari section takes a closer look.
